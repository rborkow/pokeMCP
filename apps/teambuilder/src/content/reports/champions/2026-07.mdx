## Where the meta stands

Regulation M-B opens with a tight, flat top tier rather than a single dominant threat. Four Pokémon cluster above 28%: Sinistcha (33.00%), Garchomp (32.30%), Basculegion (30.34%), and Incineroar (28.35%). Whimsicott (26.67%) and Kingambit (23.09%) round out the pillars, with Farigiraf (21.61%) the last name above 20%.

The shape here is a Trick Room/redirection axis (Sinistcha, Farigiraf) coexisting with fast offense (Garchomp, Basculegion, the Mega Charizard-Y and Mega Staraptor pair at 15.32% and 15.14%). Incineroar remains the universal glue — it not only sits at 28.35% on ladder but appeared in the top-cut bring rates of every listed tournament (9/16 at St. Jude, 5/8 at both INGAGE and Tenki's), and made every winning team. Fairy-typed support is dense: Whimsicott, Grimmsnarl (15.02%), Mega Floette (14.90%), and Sylveon (12.82%) all populate the top 17, so screens/Tailwind and redirection are baked into the format's speed control.

Restricted/legendary usage is notably absent from the top 20 — this is a Mega-driven format, with six Mega Evolutions (Charizard-Y, Staraptor, Floette, Swampert, Metagross, Mawile) between #11 and #19.

## Where it's heading

Only one month is on record for Regulation M-B (2026-06), so there is no trend line to extrapolate — every figure here is a snapshot, not a slope. Treat month-over-month calls as premature until a second data point lands.

What the tournament data *does* suggest, as a leading indicator ahead of ladder: Incineroar and Basculegion are over-performing their raw usage in top cut (Incineroar in three winning cores; Basculegion 5/8 at INGAGE, and the INGAGE winner's Choice Scarf Basculegion lead). Sneasler, only 9th on ladder at 17.24%, punched above weight with 7/16, 2/8, and 4/8 bring rates — a candidate to climb as the ladder catches up to what strong players are already bringing. Sinistcha leads ladder usage but converted more modestly in top cut (3/8, 3/8), so its 33.00% may prove soft.

## What to prepare for

- **Incineroar is non-negotiable to plan around.** At 28.35% and present on every winning team plus every tournament's top bring rates, assume it faces you most games — bring Intimidate insurance and pressure for the Fake Out/pivot slot.
- **Respect the Fairy support glut.** Whimsicott (26.67%), Grimmsnarl (15.02%), Mega Floette (14.90%), and Sylveon (12.82%) mean Tailwind, screens, and Fairy STAB are everywhere; Steel and Poison answers (Kingambit, Gholdengo, Sneasler) earn their slots.
- **Cover the fast-offense duo.** Garchomp (32.30%) and Basculegion (30.34%) headline the offensive core — the INGAGE winner ran Choice Scarf Basculegion as a lead. Have a Wave Crash/Last Respects check and Garchomp answers ready.
- **Don't under-prepare Sneasler.** Its 17.24% ladder share undersells consistent top-cut representation (up to 4/8); a Dire Claw + Unburden threat that outruns much of the format.

## Common questions

### Is Incineroar still the best support Pokémon in Regulation M-B?

By the data, yes — it's #4 on ladder at 28.35% and appeared in all three listed tournament winners' teams plus the highest or joint-highest top-cut bring rate in every event (9/16, 5/8, 5/8). Nothing in the current numbers challenges its role as the format's default pivot.

### What is the best lead in Champions M-B right now?

Tournament results point to fast offense over setup: the INGAGE winner led Choice Scarf Basculegion (30.34% usage), and Garchomp (32.30%) is the second-most-used Pokémon overall. Both are top-three ladder picks and reward aggressive turn-one pressure.

### Is Sinistcha overrated at #1?

It's genuinely the most-used Pokémon at 33.00%, but it converted less impressively in top cut (3/8 at INGAGE, 3/8 at Tenki's) than its ladder ranking implies. It's a strong Trick Room/support piece, but the tournament data suggests raw usage overstates its edge over the field.
