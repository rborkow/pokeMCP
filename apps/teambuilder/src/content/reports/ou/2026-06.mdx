## Where the meta stands

Great Tusk remains the format's defining presence at 34.93% (+0.62), nearly seven points clear of the field. Gholdengo continues its climb to 27.76% (+1.32), now firmly the #2 pick, with Dragonite (24.85%, +0.17), Kingambit (24.68%, -0.07) and Zamazenta (24.26%, +0.92) tightly packed behind. This top five defines the backbone every team must answer: a ground-type breaker/hazard setter, a spin-blocking ghost, two priority-wielding setup threats, and a bulky steel pivot.

The midfield is dense, with Ogerpon-Wellspring (18.16%, +0.68), Iron Valiant (15.52%), Raging Bolt (15.50%), Ting-Lu (15.40%) and Hatterene (15.20%) all within a point of each other. The notable shift this month is at the back end: Samurott-Hisui jumped to 13.96% (+2.48) while Kyurem (14.56%, -1.50), Pecharunt (10.85%, -1.67), Corviknight (10.70%, -1.37) and Dragapult (11.58%, -2.01) all slid. Ladder volume dropped from ~1.14M to ~942K battles.

## Where it's heading

- **Gholdengo and Dragonite look set to keep rising.** Gholdengo has climbed every month since February (21.47% → 23.25% → 26.44% → 27.76%), and Dragonite has gained steadily since December (19.06% up to 24.85%). Both trends are intact and point upward.
- **Samurott-Hisui is the clearest riser.** After hovering near 10–12% for months, it broke out to 13.96%, its highest on record. If the spike reflects a real shift rather than noise, expect continued presence in the low-to-mid teens.
- **Dragapult is in sustained decline.** It has fallen four straight months from a February peak of 15.62% down to 11.58%. The trend suggests it may keep sliding unless its tools are re-evaluated.
- **Zamazenta has recovered sharply.** From a March low of 18.99% it rebounded to 23.34% and now 24.26%, suggesting renewed favor that could hold.
- **Volcarona is the wildcard.** It appears for the first time in the record at 9.22%, debuting straight into relevance. With no prior history, treat it as an emerging threat rather than an established trend — but a 9%+ debut warrants attention.

## What to prepare for

- **The Gholdengo problem is growing.** At 27.76% and rising, Gholdengo demands reliable answers to its spin-blocking and special pressure. Hazard control needs to function through it — Great Tusk at 34.93% is the most common answer, but plan for the ghost to wall passive removal.
- **Account for a new fire-type breaker.** Volcarona's 9.22% debut means a Quiver Dance sweeper is now in the builder's calculus. Your defensive backbone should not be blind to it — note that Corviknight (10.70%, -1.37) and Hatterene are common but Volcarona pressures both.
- **Priority and steel coverage stays mandatory.** Kingambit (24.68%) and Dragonite (24.85%) remain top-four win conditions. Bulky checks and faster scarfed answers are non-negotiable given both sit near a quarter of all teams.
- **Don't over-invest in Dragapult answers.** With Dragapult down to 11.58% and trending lower, dedicated tech for it is lower priority than it was a few months ago; reallocate toward the rising Samurott-Hisui (13.96%) and Volcarona.

## Common questions

### Is Dragapult still worth running in Gen 9 OU?

Dragapult has declined four months straight, from a February peak of 15.62% to 11.58% in May (-2.01 this month). It's still a top-16 pick and viable, but the steady slide suggests it's losing favor relative to other speed-control and pivot options.

### Why is Samurott-Hisui rising?

Samurott-Hisui posted the single biggest gain among established Pokémon, jumping +2.48 points to 13.96% — its highest mark on record after months around 10–12%. The data only shows the spike, not the cause, but it has clearly broken out of its prior range and is one to watch.

### Should I prepare for Volcarona?

Yes. Volcarona debuted in the usage record at 9.22% in May, having been absent (below 2%) every prior month. That's a meaningful first appearance, so factoring a Quiver Dance fire threat into your defensive core is now worthwhile, even though there's no longer-term trend to lean on yet.
