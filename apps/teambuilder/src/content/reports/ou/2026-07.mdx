## Where the meta stands

Great Tusk remains the format's centerpiece at 32.83%, but it slipped -2.10 pts and is off its 35%+ peak from March. Behind it, the top tier is a familiar wall of Steel and Fairy-adjacent threats: Gholdengo 27.20% (-0.56), Kingambit 23.89% (-0.79), and Zamazenta 22.42% (-1.84). The headline story is the collapse of the setup sweepers that had been climbing all spring — Dragonite cratered -5.10 pts to 19.75%, and Volcarona vanished entirely (9.22% → 0.00%), a swing large enough to reshape the offensive landscape.

Filling that vacuum are special attackers and pivots on the rise. Iron Valiant (17.73%, +2.21) and Kyurem (17.16%, +2.59) both jumped, Cinderace surged +3.45 to 13.30%, and Glimmora climbed +2.86 to 11.43%. On the defensive side, Alomomola gained +2.40 to 11.98% and Slowking-Galar rose +1.26 to 13.18%, signaling a bulkier, pivot-heavy backbone forming underneath the offense.

## Where it's heading

No prior scorecard was supplied, so these are fresh calls.

The clearest trend is **Cresselia**, which more than doubled from 2.60% to 5.70% — its series (2.14 → 2.78 → 2.60 → 5.70) shows a hard break upward alongside a rebuilt kit (Lunar Dance/Eject Button giving way to Moonlight/Moonblast/Calm Mind with Leftovers 36.3% and Covert Cloak 22.5%). This looks like a genuinely new role finding traction and has room to keep climbing. **Kyurem** has trended up four of six months and now sits at a series high 17.16%; with Choice Specs share rising to 29.1%, it should hold or extend. **Iron Valiant** rebounded to 17.73% and its move to more Choice Specs (12.5% → 18.0%) and Calm Mind sets suggests continued relevance.

On the downside, **Dragonite** at 19.75% has broken a months-long climb; extrapolating from the -5.10 drop, further slippage is plausible if the meta stays hostile to setup. **Great Tusk** has now declined two straight months from its March 35.69% peak, though at 32.83% it remains the pillar and any "decline" is off an enormous base.

## What to prepare for

- **The setup-sweeper threat has softened, but special offense is ascendant.** With Dragonite and Volcarona receding and Kyurem, Iron Valiant, and Cinderace rising, prioritize special walls and answers to Booster/Specs breakers over dedicated Extreme Speed/priority checks.
- **Hazard warfare is heating up.** Glimmora (+2.86, now leaning Red Card 46.0% / Focus Sash 23.5% with Stealth Rock at 19.6% of its sets) and Cinderace's steady Court Change (22.3%) both spiked; pair reliable removal (Great Tusk, Iron Treads, Corviknight all top-18) with your own hazard pressure.
- **The defensive backbone is trending bulky-pivot.** Alomomola (11.98%, Assault Vest up to 51.4%) and Slowking-Galar (13.18%) are gaining as regenerator pivots. Build around wearing these down or breaking past their recovery rather than racing them.
- **Cresselia is now a real presence at 5.70%** with Calm Mind sets — don't assume it's a niche Trick Room/Lunar Dance body anymore; it can win the long game against passive cores.

## Common questions

### Is Dragonite still worth using in Gen 9 OU?

Dragonite fell -5.10 pts to 19.75%, its first real break after climbing from 20.36% (Jan) to a 24.85% peak in May. Its build is stable — Dragon Dance, Earthquake, and Extreme Speed still split its moveset and Heavy-Duty Boots ticked up to 64.8% — so the drop reads as a metagame shift away from setup rather than a nerf to the set. It's still a top-5 threat, just no longer the runaway climber it was.

### Why is Cinderace usage climbing again?

Cinderace jumped +3.45 to 13.30%, reversing a two-month slide (12.24% Mar → 9.84% May). Its Heavy-Duty Boots share rose from 82.4% to 87.8% and U-turn edged ahead of Court Change, pointing to heavier use as a hazard-immune pivot in the current hazard-heavy climate. It's a beneficiary of the same trend lifting Glimmora and defensive pivots.

### What happened to Volcarona?

Volcarona went from 9.22% in May to 0.00% in June — a complete disappearance from the usage table. The data only shows it present for a single month (May), so treat this as a full dropout rather than a gradual decline. Its exit is the single largest mover in the dataset and directly opens space for the special attackers now climbing.
